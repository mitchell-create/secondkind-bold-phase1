## THE VOICE (the heart of it)

**Territory we own:** the only gut-health brand that sides with the customer's frustration instead of managing it — speaking in the same blunt, self-aware register as someone venting to their most informed friend, then delivering the mechanism receipt that makes the vent make sense.

**How we sound:** the vindicated insider — someone who went through the same years of wasted money, names the mechanism failure without flinching, and hands you the proof before you ask for it. Dry, not warm. Direct, not aggressive. The friend who finally explains why your probiotic never worked, and happens to have a peer-reviewed citation in her back pocket.

**The structural move (the four-beat arc — use it on every piece):**
1. **Open in the customer's exact register** — the specific, slightly embarrassing lived moment (the 7pm bloat, the third bottle that did nothing, the 2pm wall). Pull this from the Unspoken-Truths Bank. This earns recognition and trust.
2. **Diagnose the mechanism** — not "here's a better product," but "here's why the last one structurally could not have worked."
3. **Vindicate** — "you weren't wrong, the product was."
4. **Close on the receipt + offer** — the clinical data, the named compound, the specific outcome, then the 60-day money-back guarantee.

**The guardrail test (apply to every line):** would the customer say this exact line about *herself*, to her most trusted friend? If the cut lands on her body, her choices, or her intelligence — it's off-side. If it lands on the industry's delivery model, the failure mechanism, or the lie she was sold — it's on-side.

**Hard rules (never break these):**
- The receipt arrives in the next line — every claim is backed immediately by a mechanism, statistic, or trial citation.
- **Never name a competitor brand.** The indictment is category-level ("the probiotics you've tried," "the live-bacteria model," "billions of CFUs"). (Internally, the competitors are Seed, Arrae, Ritual, Garden of Life — never in customer-facing copy.)
- Never mock the customer's past purchases or imply she was naive.
- Never bash doctors — frame as "literature ahead of practice."
- Never lead with operational claims (guarantee, refund, subscription) — those close, they don't open.
- FDA: bold language in headlines/body; "helps support" / "may help" qualifiers in fine print only. No: cure, treats, heals, prevents, reverses, eliminates.
- No hype words: miracle, revolutionary, game-changer, transform your, journey, ritual, find your best self, unlock your, begin your.
- No scarcity / urgency / countdowns — a weak, off-voice lever for this whole audience.
- Body/appearance language only when the blame lands on the mechanism — and **never** for Natalie, Paul, or Isaac (see their notes).

Whatever the format, **open from the Unspoken-Truths Bank and run the four-beat arc.**
